# Gold set — brief for the reader

A short brief you can send to a candidate. It describes a discrete, paid,
remote piece of work with a clear deliverable.

Everything below is honest about what the work is and why it matters,
because the person who does it well is the person who understands that the
answer is the point, not the throughput.

---

## The role, in one line

Read 100 Texas court opinions and record, for each, whether the court
actually decided a question about a particular kind of contract clause.

## Who this suits

- A US-qualified attorney, or a law student or recent JD comfortable with
  Texas commercial litigation
- Able to read an appellate opinion and separate a holding from dicta,
  from a procedural disposition, and from a party's argument
- No AI or technical experience needed. This is legal reading.

## The work

We are building a reference of contract clauses that courts have refused
to enforce. Software reads the opinions and proposes an answer. **Your
100 cases are the yardstick we measure the software against** — the
ground truth. If the machine agrees with you often enough, it can be
trusted at scale; if it does not, we fix it before it goes anywhere near
a lawyer's desk.

It follows that the software must never see your answers first, and your
answers cannot be produced with its help. Grading the machine with the
machine measures nothing.

## The doctrine

Texas's **express negligence** rule:

> An indemnity or release covering the indemnitee's own negligence is
> unenforceable unless it says so expressly and is conspicuous.

*Ethyl Corp. v. Daniel Construction Co.*, 725 S.W.2d 705 (Tex. 1987);
*Dresser Industries, Inc. v. Page Petroleum, Inc.*, 853 S.W.2d 505 (Tex.
1993).

## For each of the 100 opinions, record

1. **Did the decision turn on whether a clause of this kind was
   enforceable?** — `on point` / `not on point` / `uncertain`
2. **One or two sentences of reasoning**, quoting the opinion where you
   can
3. **If on point:** the clause as the court reproduced it (copy it
   verbatim), and what the court did with it — enforced, refused,
   enforced in part
4. **Anything that made it hard.** Split holdings, a procedural
   disposition that reads like a merits ruling, an opinion that discusses
   the doctrine without deciding it. These are the most valuable notes
   you can give us.

## What we especially need you to be strict about

- **A dissent or concurrence decided nothing.** If the document is a
  separate writing, say so.
- **Deciding a clause *was* enforceable is on point too.** A record of
  failures with no successes is a collection of anecdotes.
- **"Uncertain" is a real answer, not a failure.** We would far rather
  have an honest uncertain than a confident guess. Nothing you mark
  uncertain counts against you.

## Practicalities

- **100 opinions**, supplied as text with a link to the source
- **Roughly 10–20 minutes each** — about 20–35 hours total
- **Remote, your own hours**, within about two weeks
- **Paid** at a professional hourly rate; we will agree the rate up front
  against the estimate above and pay for the hours the work actually takes
- Delivered as a spreadsheet; we can supply the template

## Why we are being this careful

The output is a reference other lawyers will rely on. If it says a court
struck a clause down and no court did, that is worse than having no
reference at all. Your 100 cases are how we find out whether we are good
enough to publish anything.

## To apply

A short note on your Texas or US commercial litigation background, and
your availability over the next two weeks.

### Where to look for this person

- **Law school career offices** — Texas schools especially (UT Austin,
  SMU, Houston, Baylor, Texas A&M); research assistantships of this shape
  are familiar to them
- **Upwork / Lawtrades / LawClerk / Hire an Esquire** — freelance legal
  research marketplaces
- **r/LawSchool, r/Lawyertalk** and state bar young-lawyer sections
- A friendly practising attorney who will take a weekend project

The cheapest good option is usually a 2L/3L or recent graduate with
litigation experience. The most reliable is a practising commercial
litigator, at a higher rate.

### One warning

Whoever it is, they must not use an AI assistant to produce the answers.
Say so explicitly and put it in writing. It is the single thing that would
invalidate the entire exercise, and it is exactly the shortcut a rushed
person would reach for.
