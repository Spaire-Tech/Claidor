# The five-walls research round — findings and dispositions

28 August 2026. The founder's research agent answered
`research-brief.md`. This records what it found and **what we did about
each item**, in the shape `china-os-findings.md` established: a
disposition per entry, not a summary.

The round separated **VERIFIED** (installed, ran, or computed in
session) from **READ** (paper or repo seen, not run). That distinction
is kept below, because it decides how much weight an item carries
before we re-measure it ourselves.

## Adopted immediately, and already landed

### 1. The `[$…]` currency bracket — DONE, 28 Aug

**Their finding (VERIFIED by them, re-verified here).** Excel's
`[$symbol-locale]` bracket contains a `$` as *syntax*. The rule: split
at the last hyphen; what precedes it is the symbol; empty means no
currency. Require a hexadecimal locale, and fall back to the first
hyphen for modern `en-US` tags.

They also measured that **`numfmt` (MIT) implements this correctly and
`ssf` (Apache-2.0) carries our exact bug** — SSF invents a dollar sign
for `[$-409]`. And that numfmt still misses *alphabetic* symbols
(`CHF`, `kr`).

**Disposition: implemented, 16 of 16 cases correct**, including both
numfmt gets wrong. Measured on `forfar_model.xlsm`: **USD 53 → EUR 53**,
and the formats hand-read to confirm the euro is genuinely there. The
SSF warning is recorded in the code so the bug cannot return by
dependency.

### 2. The coincidence control was the wrong statistic — DONE, 28 Aug

**Their finding (VERIFIED by them, reproduced here).** Two facts, both
arithmetic:
- With zero matches in `d` pairs the exact one-sided 95% upper bound is
  `1 − 0.05^(1/d)`. **59 pairs are needed to claim « under 5% »**; 0 of
  2 is consistent with 77.6%.
- A permutation test on `n` rows has `n!` arrangements, so the smallest
  reachable p is `1/n!` — **0.5 at n = 2**. Below n = 4 the question is
  not askable.
- Scoring sixteen small samples separately makes one breach of a 5%
  bar the *expected* outcome even at a true rate of 2%.

**Disposition: criterion 1 restated and re-measured pooled.** Result:
30 of 10,827 mismatched pairs, **0.376% at the exact 95% bound**, where
I had reported three models as failures. Their three published values
(30/10,827 → 0.376%, 0/59 → 4.95%, 0/2 → 77.6%) all reproduce against
our implementation, which is how a bug in *our* bound was caught.

## Adopted, queued, not yet built

### 3. Format-decidable unit findings — the best new candidate

**Their finding (VERIFIED by them against `numfmt`).** Two defect
classes are decidable **from the number-format string alone, with no
inference at all**:

- `0.0"%"` prints a percent sign but does **not** multiply by 100, so a
  reader sees 0.1% where the model means 5%. A quoted `"%"` literal
  where a real `%` token belongs is a finding.
- Trailing commas each divide the displayed value by a thousand
  (`#,##0,` thousands, `#,##0,,` millions), so « thousands mixed with
  millions » is **provable**, not inferred.

**Why this matters more than it looks.** Every units check we have
tried is blocked on the inference's accuracy, and E3a was refused
precisely because currency failed a second key at 24.62%. **These two
classes have no accuracy to fail.** They read a declaration the file
makes about itself.

**Disposition: registered as its own round before any code.** It goes
ahead of further work on the inference, because it is the only part of
the units family that can ship without waiting on a second
hand-labelled key that nobody has drawn.

### 4. XBRL `periodType` as a lexicon for flow vs stock

**Their finding (READ).** XBRL requires every concept in every taxonomy
to declare `instant` or `duration` — our stock and flow — so US-GAAP,
IFRS and the rest are already labelled by the people who define the
accounting. They also found that **XBRL's own calculation linkbase
cannot express a roll-forward**, because the balances are instant and
the changes are duration; it needs a separate assertion, and their
Calculations 2.0 requirements list it as a distinct type.

**Disposition: filed as a prior, not a dependency.** Our
`classify_row` reads kind from behaviour because our files declare
nothing, and that inference is the part with no prior work. A taxonomy
could seed it, and their caution is recorded: **note and policy
concepts default to `duration` on a technical argument**, so a taxonomy
prior is trustworthy on primary statements and misleading in the notes.

The roll-forward point is also a **sales line**: the ordinary
summation checks every seat tool ships structurally cannot do what this
check does, and a standards body had to build separate machinery for
it.

### 5. AggreCol — values-only aggregation recovery

**Their finding (VERIFIED — cloned, rebuilt, and run).** Jiang,
Vitagliano, Hameed, Naumann (HPI, EDBT 2022), **Apache-2.0**, at
`HPI-Information-Systems/AggreCol`. Finds arithmetic relations from
values with no formulas.

They rebuilt its core detector and ran it on project-finance shapes:

| cells | seconds | genuine | spurious |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 320,000 | 21.5 | 69 | 208,506 |
| 600,000 | 48.3 | 105 | 390,202 |

**Speed is not the problem** — 600k cells in 48 s of plain Python.
**Precision is**: ~3,700 spurious per real one. And they traced it:
**99.9% of raw candidates have a zero in the target cell.** Remove
those and it is 71 genuine against 119 spurious — 37% precision.

They also measured that **AggreCol's own coincidence control breaks on
our files**: its coverage threshold of 0.70 keeps *nothing* on a
financial model, because our sheets deliberately mix flows, balances,
rates and flags in the same columns, so a real pattern can never cover
most rows. The genuine total-column pattern covered 8.9%.

**Disposition: filed for Wall 1, not started, and the zero rule
adopted in principle now.** We already exclude zero-against-zero in
`periods.py` for exactly this reason, arrived at independently by
hand-reading Kelso — which is a useful corroboration that the failure
mode is real and general rather than a quirk of our data.

The **fact to take to a buyer** is theirs and it is good: of 200 real
spreadsheets they examined, **150 contained an aggregation and not one
of the 150 contained the formula for it.**

## Where they confirmed there is nothing

Recorded so we stop looking, and because a proven absence is worth as
much as a find.

1. **No published work on values-only structure recovery aimed at
   financial models.** AggreCol targets statistics CSVs and its
   parameters are wrong for us.
2. **No coincidence control from the pattern-mining literature has ever
   been applied to spreadsheet auditing.** The machinery is thirty
   years old and unused in our field. The route in is Hämäläinen &
   Webb (2019), *A tutorial on statistically sound pattern discovery*,
   and — for our exact case of discrete p-values with resolution
   varying per test — Döhler, Durand & Roquain (2018), with the R
   package `DiscreteFDR` as a specification to port.
3. **No method or benchmark for a table split horizontally across two
   facing pages** with labels on one and numbers on the other. That is
   the Chain's exact case and the work is ours.
4. **No released spreadsheet unit-inference tool in any language, and
   no research since 2020.** They checked the authors' own publication
   lists: the Microsoft group behind the 2020 paper moved wholesale to
   LLM work and never returned. Six years, same group, no successor.
5. **No spreadsheet-auditing paper acknowledges formula-free workbooks
   as a population.** Every approach assumes formulas and stops when
   there are none.

Item 5 is the sales line, and it is now measured on both sides: three
of our four financial-close models are unreadable by every published
spreadsheet-auditing method, and nobody in that field has noticed.

## The one that changes how the Chain is read

**PubTables-v2** — Smock et al., Kensho Technologies, arXiv:2512.10888.
Dataset **CDLA-Permissive-2.0**, code and models **MIT**. 548,414
tables annotated in-page, **9,492 multi-page tables**, and 630
single-page split tables.

**The measured result: on split tables, exact-match content accuracy
was 0.000 for every one of seven models tested.** Best structural
accuracy 0.0625.

**Disposition: this reframes the Chain's six failed rounds.** They are
not a competence problem. The closest published analogue to our case is
comprehensively failed by everything currently shipping, and our case —
a wide table across two *facing* pages — is harder than the one they
benchmarked.

Two things to use:
- Their **continuation detector** — a plain ViT-B-16 on two page
  images reaching F1 0.991 — is the one component that works. It merges
  *vertically* and only when column counts match, so the architecture
  transfers and the weights probably do not (their corpus is English
  scientific articles; ours is redacted 359-page project agreements).
- **docling issue #2976**, open since February 2026: a table spanning
  pages is emitted as separate tables and never merged. **Verified by
  reading the tracker.** Not a misconfiguration at our end — the merge
  does not exist.
