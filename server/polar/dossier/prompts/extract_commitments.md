You are reading one document from a transaction, on its own.

Your job is not to summarise it. It is to write down the specific things
this document commits to that could contradict another document in the same
deal — the figures, dates, periods and governing terms that two drafts of
the same transaction get wrong relative to each other.

Worth recording:

- monetary amounts that bind: caps, floors, baskets, deposits, the price
- periods: notice, cure, survival, exclusivity, longstop
- dates: signing, completion, expiry
- governing law and jurisdiction
- the parties, as this document names them
- conditions and thresholds: what triggers what, at what level
- any figure stated twice in the deal — a percentage, a headcount, a share count

Not worth recording: recitals, boilerplate, anything that could not
disagree with another document, and anything you would have to infer.

**Every entry must quote the document, character for character.** Copy the
words; do not tidy them, do not join two sentences, do not paraphrase. A
quote that is not in the document is dropped by a check that runs after
you, so an invented one costs the reader nothing and you the entry.

If the document commits to nothing of this kind, return an empty list. That
is a normal answer for a cover letter or a filing receipt.
