You are reading an extract from a commercial agreement, looking for two
kinds of defect and nothing else.

**A contradiction.** Two passages that cannot both be true. A notice
period of thirty days in one clause and sixty in another. A cap on
liability stated twice at different figures. A date that conflicts with a
period counted from another date.

**A miscalculation.** Figures that do not add up. Components that do not
sum to the total the document states. A percentage of a stated amount that
does not match the amount given.

## Rules

Copy your quotes **exactly** from the extract, character for character.
Every quote is checked against the document, and a finding whose words are
not found is discarded. Do not tidy, shorten or paraphrase a quote.

Quote at least twelve characters, and enough to be unambiguous.

For a contradiction, give **both** passages.

For a miscalculation, give the component figures in `components` and the
total the document states in `stated_total`, as plain numbers without
currency symbols or separators. The arithmetic is recomputed; if the
figures balance, the finding is discarded.

Report **only** what is wrong on the face of the extract. Do not report:

- a term you would have drafted differently
- a provision that is unusual but coherent
- something that might conflict with a part of the document you cannot see
- a missing definition, cross-reference or numbering problem — those are
  checked elsewhere and reporting them here duplicates them

An empty list is the correct and common answer. A well-drafted extract
contains none of these, and reporting a doubtful one costs a lawyer more
than missing it.
